The Core Story
Contracts calls are not fundamentally different in seller demographics or property type. The difference is what agents do in the conversation. Contracts agents push for appointments more, offer flexibility more, and say "as-is" explicitly more — and they get sellers to say "sounds good" 10 percentage points more often.

Agent Move #1 — The Double Winner

Appointment Push — +7pp C vs NC, +5pp CL vs Dead

28% of contract calls vs 21% of no-contract calls. And 30% of Closed deals vs 25% of Dead deals. The only agent move that wins on BOTH dimensions.
AGENT — Indianapolis ✅ CLOSED
"We had an agent come out and look — he said list for one seventy. But let me tell you what happens after you list it: you pay realtor commissions AND you still have to fix it up. Let me send someone out this week so we can walk it together."
SELLER ↗
"Okay now when can you send someone?"
AGENT — SF Bay Area ✅ CLOSED
"I can't give you a number on this call — every house in this area is different. I want to schedule one of our area experts to come walk the property. He's been in this market for years."
SELLER ↗
"That'd be alright. Sounds good."
AGENT — SF Bay Area Contracts
"He's the person buying all our houses in San Jose. He can meet you there — I have him available Tuesday at ten. He'll walk through, see the condition, and sit with you to make a fair offer."
SELLER ↗
"Ten AM Monday works actually for us. Yes."
Why Appointment Push Wins Both
It's a commitment device. Once a seller agrees to a walkthrough, the deal has momentum. No-contract agents often skip pushing for the visit — they wait for the seller to ask. Contract agents create the moment instead of waiting for it.
The Language Pattern
Closed agents say: "I have someone in the area" / "Our expert can be there Tuesday" / "Let me schedule the walkthrough." They name a specific time, a specific person. Dead agents say: "We can set something up." Vague vs concrete = no deal vs deal.
Agent Move #2 — The Close-Specific Unlock

Flexible Timeline — Only +2pp C vs NC, but +7pp Closed vs Dead

This move isn't what separates contracts from no-contracts — it's what separates closed deals from dead ones. 13% of Closed calls vs only 6% of Dead calls contain it.
CLOSED (13% of 393 deals)
"We can be very very flexible on the time frame to close if we agree on the price."

"You don't have to rush. Could be 30 days, could be 90 — you choose."

"On your schedule, on your terms."
DEAD (only 6% of 531 deals)
Agents in Dead deals either:

(a) Never mentioned timeline flexibility, or

(b) Mentioned it after price, when the seller was already anchored on rejecting the number — too late to dissolve the objection.
AGENT — SF Bay Area ✅ CLOSED
"We can be very very flexible on the time frame. If we reach an agreement — we can give you money at closing, and once you're gone your check is ready."
SELLER ↗
"Okay that works perfectly. Yes."
AGENT — Kansas City ✅ CLOSED
"We cover fees, commissions, closing costs — and give you that sixty-to-ninety day flexible time frame. You own it until then."
SELLER ↗
"Give me your best shot and we'll go from there."
Why it closes (but doesn't create) deals
Flexible timeline doesn't pull sellers off the fence — it seals deals for sellers already leaning toward yes. The fear of being rushed is the last invisible objection. When it's named and removed, sellers stop stalling. Use it after empathy and as-is, not before.
Agent Moves #3 & #4

"As-Is" and Empathy — Consistent Across Both Dimensions

✅ "As-Is" Explicit — C:17% vs NC:14% (+3pp) | CL:20% vs D:15% (+5pp)
Saying "we buy it as-is" out loud cancels the mental repair budget sellers are running. They've been calculating "$15K roof + $8K plumbing..." — the moment they hear "as-is" that math disappears. Works both for getting a contract AND for getting that contract to close.
✅ Empathy Before Business — C:7% vs NC:4% (+3pp) | CL:8% vs D:5% (+3pp)
One sentence acknowledging the seller's situation before asking about the house. "Before we talk numbers, I'm sorry to hear that — that sounds like a lot." Contracts agents do this 3pp more. And within contracts, it appears 3pp more in Closed deals.

"Eric, before we talk about any business or numbers — I want to tell you I'm sorry for your loss. I know you're going through a lot."
SELLER ↗
"I appreciate that, Raul. Yeah — let's do this."

Counterintuitive Findings

What Doesn't Work — The Surprises

Three things that seem like they'd predict closing — but don't. From the data.
⚠ Curiosity / Questions
"How does this work? What's the process? What's the catch?"

Appears in 13% of Dead deals vs only 9% of Closed. High-curiosity sellers are shopping or skeptical, not converting. Don't mistake "lots of questions" for "serious interest."
⚠ Price Acceptance
"That's fair / I can work with that / That sounds reasonable."

Appears in 12% of Dead deals vs 10% of Closed. Sellers who verbally accept price don't always close. Often means they're anchoring for a later negotiation.
⚠ Offer / Price Mentioned by Agent
Agents mentioning a specific $ number appears in 17% of Dead deals vs 15% of Closed. Pricing without prior relationship/empathy creates anchoring and friction. Price is not the unlock — comfort is.
Cash / No Lender → Seller Shift (precede analysis)
When agent mentions "cash / no lender" right before a seller says "sounds good" — it shows up in 7 Dead deals vs only 1 Closed deal. Relying on "we're cash buyers" as the unlock isn't working. Sellers hear it too much.
Trust Signals Don't Predict Closing
"I like the way you talk." "You seem honest." — Appears slightly more in Dead deals (6%) than Closed (5%). Sellers can like the rep and still not sell. Warmth enables the conversation; it doesn't close it. Movement does.

The Core Difference
Contracts agents run steps 1–6 in order. No-contract agents skip steps 1–2 and jump into features (cash, no lender, price) before the seller has emotionally engaged. The seller hasn't felt heard yet — so the features land on skeptical ears.
Tactical Application

The In-Call Playbook — Based on Real Data

⭐ Say These — Before Price, Before Process
"Before we get into anything — I'm sorry to hear that, that sounds like a lot."
"Our process is very simple — just a few basic questions, takes 5 minutes."
"We buy it as-is — no repairs, no cleaning, no fixing anything."
"We're very flexible on the time frame — you choose the closing date."
⭐ Push for Appointment Concretely
Not: "We can set something up."
Say: "I have someone in your area available Tuesday at 10. Can we confirm that?"

Name a person. Name a time. Name a day. Vague appointment pushes produce no-contracts.
When Seller Asks Lots of Process Questions
"Great — let me answer all of that right after I understand your property better. Two minutes, then I give you real answers for your situation, not generic ones."

Redirect to property → surface urgency → then answer their questions with context.
When Seller Mentions Other Offers / Companies
"I can't speak for other companies. Some make sight-unseen offers — then adjust at closing. When we walk the property, the number is the number. No surprises."

Competitor contrast appears +1pp in contracts. Use it to differentiate, not attack.
When Seller Shows Price Resistance
Don't push the number. Push the timeline flexibility: "We can structure it so you have 90-120 days — the house is yours until you're ready. What would that be worth to you?" Price resistance often dissolves when urgency is re-framed.
Universal Opener to Surface Urgency
"Before anything — what's making this feel like the right time to think about selling?"

This one question surfaces life-event turning points that agents would otherwise never hear.
