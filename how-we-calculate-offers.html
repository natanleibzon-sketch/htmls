The honest answer

We pay less than what you'd list for. Here's exactly why — and how much.

Cash buyers — including us — offer less than what a home would sell for on the open market after it's been fully repaired and staged. That gap exists for a real reason. This page explains the math so you can decide whether a cash offer makes sense for your situation.

The short version: A cash offer = what your house could sell for once fixed up, minus the cost to fix it up, minus selling and carrying costs, minus the buyer's operating margin. What surprises most sellers is how small that gap is once you compare it to what listing actually costs you.

Most sellers compare the cash offer to their Zillow number and feel like they're leaving money on the table. The real comparison is what you'd actually net after repairs, commissions, and the 60–120 days it takes to close a traditional sale. That number looks different.

What moves the number

What makes your offer higher or lower

Five factors drive nearly every cash offer. Here's how each one works and what it means for your number.

🔨

Condition and repairs needed

The biggest factor. A house needing a full kitchen and new roof gets a lower offer than one that needs paint and carpet. We estimate repairs conservatively — we're taking on the risk if the number runs high.

📍

Location and local demand

What updated homes in your specific neighborhood are selling for sets the ceiling on what we can offer. Strong, fast-moving markets allow higher offers; softer markets require tighter math.

📅

Your timeline

A 7-day close and a 90-day close produce different carrying cost calculations. We build your timeline into the offer — whichever direction works for you.

⚖️

Title and legal complexity

Inherited homes, properties in probate, homes with liens or multiple owners on title take more time and cost to close. We handle all of these situations — they factor into what we can offer.

🏘️

Current market conditions

In a strong seller's market where renovated homes move fast, we can pay more. In a slower market where updated homes sit longer, our margins tighten and offers adjust accordingly.

🔑

Occupancy situation

Vacant homes are simpler and typically receive higher offers. Tenant-occupied homes — especially those with complicated lease situations — carry additional costs we factor in.

Is it right for you?

When a cash offer makes sense — and when it doesn't

✓ A cash offer usually makes sense when…

  • The house needs significant repairs you don't want to manage
  • You're handling an inherited property from out of state
  • You have a time-sensitive situation — a move, a medical need, a foreclosure date
  • You want certainty — no strangers, no showings, no risk of the deal falling through
  • The process matters as much as the price — you want it handled, not managed

Listing is probably better when…

  • Your home is in move-in-ready condition
  • You have 60–90 days and no urgency
  • Maximizing the sale price is your top priority
  • You're comfortable with showings, negotiations, and the uncertainty of a traditional sale

We'll tell you honestly which path fits your situation.
